# Removing material without removing meaning

Use a proposition inventory before selecting a length budget. Count comparisons
are useful only when they use the same representation and context boundary.

## Equivalent-claim merge

- **Use when:** Repeated statements impose the same actor/action/condition/strength.
  Replace the repetition with one statement carrying every distinct qualifier.
- **Trade-off:** Similar vocabulary can hide different scopes; a shared sentence
  may broaden an obligation or lose the actor to which it originally applied.
- **How to verify:** Map each original proposition into the candidate and exercise
  a case where actor or condition differs before accepting the merge.

## Framing deletion before qualifier deletion

- **Use when:** Introductory material repeats what a direct statement already says.
  Remove non-propositional framing while retaining exceptions and required context.
- **Trade-off:** Some framing locates audience or scope; deleting it can change
  what the remaining statement appears to require.
- **How to verify:** Compare negation, quantifiers, scope and exception cases; keep
  material whose removal makes the candidate's implication different.

## Total representation-cost check

- **Use when:** A shorter passage adds abbreviations or moves context elsewhere.
  Include definitions and necessary lookups in the proposed representation boundary.
- **Trade-off:** Reuse can justify a reference, but a local word-count win does not
  establish less material for an audience that must now read another artifact.
- **How to verify:** Count with one explicit rule, retain the context inventory,
  and report both local reduction and displaced required material.

An illustrative prose target is 10% fewer whitespace-separated words;
confirm with the user/stakeholder. Reject meaning loss even if the target is met;
a preservation-first edit may legitimately miss a proposed reduction budget.
