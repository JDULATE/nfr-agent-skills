# Response-control practices

Keep signal, authority, intervention and actual capability effects separate.

## Signal-to-activity correlation

- Use when responders need to decide which ongoing activity a signal describes.
- Trade-off: broad signals can prompt excessive intervention; narrow signals may
  miss another path. Preserve uncertainty instead of inventing a confirmed actor.
- Verify: correlate synthetic activity, identity/resource scope and signal context;
  inspect false or ambiguous signals without automatically widening response authority.

## Capability-scoped containment

- Use when credentials, sessions, queued work or alternate routes can act independently.
- Trade-off: disabling a broad service may interrupt legitimate work while a
  narrower control can leave other adversarial capabilities available.
- Verify: activate only granted controls, then attempt continuation through each
  claimed capability; inspect actual effects, not just the control receipt.

## Evidence and collateral preservation

- Use when cleanup or containment can erase needed records or block essential work.
- Trade-off: retaining evidence and minimizing collateral effects can constrain
  intervention choices; the authorized response owner must resolve genuine conflicts.
- Verify: inspect evidence availability and valid-work controls after activation;
  retain residual capabilities and any unapproved effect as explicit findings.

## Conditional reversal

- Use when containment needs to be lifted or modified after the event.
- Trade-off: permanent restriction can harm normal operations, while early reopening
  can restore the adversarial path. Green health alone decides neither condition.
- Verify: exercise confirmed clearance criteria, restored grants and attempted
  continuation; record which capability/authority actually changed at each step.
