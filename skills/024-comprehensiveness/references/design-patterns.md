# Coverage-mapping practices

Start from audience decisions; do not treat any universal topic list as mandatory.

## Question-to-subject map

- Use when the current guide's scope may omit whole reader concerns.
- Trade-off: asking broader questions can expand work; only retain subjects tied
  to the intended audience and decisions, with unresolved needs visible.
- Verify: record the question source, required subject, explanation location,
  and what decision the material enables; detect missing domains, not just headings.

## Contrasting journey review

- Use when setup or success dominates existing documentation.
- Trade-off: failure, recovery, and handoff review reveals breadth gaps but does
  not establish that every possible audience journey was explored.
- Verify: walk actual source-supported paths and compare the questions they create
  with existing explanations; keep uninspected contexts in the limitations.

## Depth and discoverability check

- Use when the topic technically appears but may not support the reader's purpose.
- Trade-off: a concise pointer avoids duplication but may fail if readers cannot
  reach or interpret the destination in their permitted context.
- Verify: follow the guide's navigation to the relevant explanation and inspect
  whether it supports the mapped decision; a bare heading receives no coverage credit.

## Exclusion ledger

- Use when the guide cannot cover every discovered subject.
- Trade-off: explicit exclusions bound the artifact honestly but leave real user
  needs unsatisfied until another artifact or scope decision addresses them.
- Verify: attach reason and reviewer to each omission and distinguish approved
  exclusions from unanswered questions; do not relabel gaps as irrelevant.
