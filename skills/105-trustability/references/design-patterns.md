# Decision-support practices

Support a specified trust decision rather than making the product sound reassuring.

## Decision and claim map

- Use when a stakeholder considers delegation, reliance or adoption.
- Trade-off: a specific decision bounds meaningful evidence; broad trusted labels
  can imply support for many actions never examined.
- Verify: identify decision owner, consequence, scoped capability claims, evidence
  and what unknowns or counterexamples could change the permitted reliance.

## Material-limit placement

- Use when documentation and the actual delegation view show different promises.
- Trade-off: concise presentation helps use, but hiding decision-changing limits
  in an appendix can leave the immediate decision unsupported.
- Verify: walk the actual decision view with its approved audience context and
  inspect whether relevant limits, unknowns and evidence are available before the choice.

## Authority and fallback boundary

- Use when an evidence card or confidence message accompanies an executable action.
- Trade-off: supported capability does not itself grant permission; an owner may
  require review or fallback even when the displayed evidence is favorable.
- Verify: compare actual granted actions with the confirmed delegation conditions;
  test unsupported cases without letting a reassuring display bypass authority.

## Calibration rather than persuasion

- Use when positive trust ratings are proposed as the success measure.
- Trade-off: candid limits may reduce willingness to delegate while improving the
  basis for the choice; preference alone does not determine justified reliance.
- Verify: compare claims with evidence and known counterexamples, retain unknowns,
  and separate artifact inspection from observed stakeholder decision quality.
