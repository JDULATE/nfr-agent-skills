# Draft evidence versus publication delegation

This fictional stakeholder is deciding whether a report tool may publish without
manual preview. Available evidence covers producing a draft from a named template;
it does not cover current recipient policy or unattended final publication.

## Claim, limit and authority inputs

Candidate wording says `Ready to publish automatically`. The actual permission
still permits draft creation only. A known unsupported case changes the report's
recipient context, which could materially affect the delegation decision.

| Input/view | Required decision-support output | Carried authority/evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Draft-production evidence | Claim limited to reviewed draft context | Revision and result scope retained |
| Unknown publication/recipient behavior | Material limit visible at delegation | No invented supporting result |
| Polished automatic-publication badge | Narrow or replace unsupported promise | Positive sentiment does not fix scope |
| Stakeholder reads evidence card | Decision support only | Draft-only permission remains in force |
| Unattended publication attempted | Follow actual granted boundary/fallback | Card does not grant authority |

## Proposed decision-review gate

Illustrative example; confirm with the user/stakeholder:
Permit 0 hidden material limitations in the agreed delegation fixtures. Ask which
limits change the real decision and who can grant publication authority.

## Concrete output and limits

Replace the broad promise with scoped draft evidence and visible publication
unknowns. Keep manual preview or the confirmed fallback pending a real decision.
Do not claim improved appropriate trust merely because the candidate looks more
reassuring or a stakeholder likes it. No actual report publication, authority
grant or informed stakeholder review occurred in this example.
