# Observer-and-linkage practices

Declare who observes what before selecting a transformation or making an identity claim.

## Observer and mapping boundary

- Use when a release replaces names with tokens or keeps an identity map elsewhere.
- Trade-off: a map can serve an approved operational purpose while making identity
  recoverable to its holders. Different observers therefore need distinct claims.
- Verify: enumerate actual mapping, export, log, account, and auxiliary access;
  test the named observer's combined view rather than a deliberately narrow demo.

## Joint context and repeated releases

- Use when role, timing, location, stable keys, or overlapping outputs supply linkage clues.
- Trade-off: removing one clue may reduce utility without removing a joint path;
  repeated views can expose links absent from an isolated release.
- Verify: join the declared accessible contexts and previous outputs, recording
  named-identity recovery separately from unnamed cross-context record linkage.

## Output granularity and utility

- Use when individual rows or unique context are unnecessary for the confirmed use.
- Trade-off: grouping, suppressing, or reducing detail can impair legitimate tasks;
  no universal group size or field-removal list is assumed sufficient.
- Verify: inspect the actual transformed output against the observer's linkage
  fixtures and the utility task; retain remaining inference paths and excluded uses.

## Claim and knowledge limits

- Use when a finite test set is proposed as proof of anonymity.
- Trade-off: bounded fixtures provide concrete counterexamples but cannot enumerate
  all future knowledge, observers, or inference strategies.
- Verify: state tested knowledge and outputs, preserve failed and successful links,
  and limit the claim to the agreed assessment rather than certifying universal anonymity.
