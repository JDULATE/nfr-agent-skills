# Meaning and interpretation patterns

Ground intended meaning in the inspected contract. These techniques explain that
meaning; they do not authorize new permissions or change an obligation.

## Actor/condition proposition map

- **Use when:** Readers disagree about who can act, which object is affected, or
  what condition limits the statement. Rewrite those relations explicitly.
- **Trade-off:** Extra words may be necessary; a shorter sentence is not preferable
  if it preserves the ambiguity or removes an essential qualification.
- **How to verify:** Map source propositions to the new statement, then ask who
  may act in included and excluded cases without supplying the actor in the question.

## Domain distinction with a contrasting case

- **Use when:** A familiar term has a narrower project meaning or readers infer a
  universal rule from examples. Define the distinction at the relevant passage.
- **Trade-off:** Too much background can obscure the local question; do not turn
  a focused clarification into an unrequested comprehensive manual.
- **How to verify:** Use a case satisfying the definition and a near case that
  does not; compare the reader's explanation with the source-backed distinction.

## Unaided interpretation protocol

- **Use when:** A rewrite claims readers now infer the intended meaning. Record
  audience familiarity, allowed context, equivalent scenarios, and a reviewed key.
- **Trade-off:** Repeated exposure or author hints can supply the answer; a convenient
  author walkthrough is source review, not independent audience evidence.
- **How to verify:** Retain first unaided answers, wrong implications, and missing
  concepts. Use fresh cases where the original and rewrite cannot be compared fairly.

An illustrative task bound is 0 incorrect interpretations on the agreed scenarios;
confirm with the user/stakeholder. Ask the user which audience, context and question
set support the claim; do not infer universal comprehensibility from a short trial.
