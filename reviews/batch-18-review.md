## Per-skill weaknesses and post-fix transfer findings

### 007 affordability

The supplied flat charging rule is deliberately synthetic.
Batching reduces the modeled total only because that rule charges per request
and the fixture supports equivalent batch results.
The alternative per-record model demonstrates that fewer calls alone do not
establish monetary savings.

Actual rates, billing periods, additional charges, and provider failure behavior
remain unverified. The ledger is not authority to spend or proof of deliverability.
A real budget boundary still needs owner confirmation.

Post-fix transfer count: 4 tested; 2 specific, 2 partly shared, 0 transferable.
Nearest siblings in bullet order: feasibility, efficiency-device-efficiency,
viability, feasibility.
Commitment attribution can inform continuation too, and preserved scope also
matters to delivery. Here the accepted result is a nonduplicated spending total
for the declared workload, not a general resource reduction or delivery promise.

### 040 effectiveness

The reconciliation fixture assumes approved counts and a permitted correction
path. It does not establish physical-count accuracy, real authorization, staff
adoption, or improvement across an operating population.

The modeled downstream correction can meet the declared stock goal while a
review-completion label cannot. This supports the fixture distinction only.
An accepted manual path is attributed separately rather than counted as evidence
that the new feature caused an improvement.

Downstream-effect confirmation was moved into Verification because it transfers
as a checking practice. Three Rules of thumb remain, below the guide's usual
four-to-six range; no filler was added to restore that count.

Post-fix transfer count: 3 tested; 1 specific, 2 partly shared, 0 transferable.
Nearest siblings in bullet order: functionality, usability, correctness.
Alternative paths also matter to task interaction, and hidden work can violate
behavioral rules. The accepted question here remains attainment of the selected
stakeholder outcome rather than feature use or a proxy completion event.

### 049 feasibility

The local-renderer probe covers a supplied template in a synthetic restricted
environment. It does not establish production integration, packaging,
availability of a real dependency, or a calendar delivery date.
Fixture work slots are declared assumptions, not productivity statistics.

Review exposed a timing gap: prerequisites and delivery capacity must overlap.
The final example identifies the renderer's availability in window amber and
rejects the candidate for amber when it is available only in later window birch.

The generic hard-case probe instruction moved into Verification.
A repeated scope-preservation rule was deleted because the unchanged deliverable
is already required by the workflow and candidate comparison.
Three retained rules concern hard environment constraints, remaining delivery
work, and a usable common prerequisite window.

Post-fix transfer count: 3 tested; 2 specific, 1 partly shared, 0 transferable.
Nearest siblings in bullet order: portability, integratability, viability.
Integration work is also an assembly concern, but a successful spike does not
remove that work from the initial deliverability decision.

### 052 functionality

An invocable handler and its declared effect are both required.
This does not show that the capability is fast, suitable for an offline receiver,
or effective at producing a business outcome.
The placeholder-cancel control demonstrates why method names and acknowledgements
are insufficient presence evidence.

Post-fix transfer count: 4 tested; 2 specific, 2 partly shared, 0 transferable.
Nearest siblings in bullet order: correctness, suitability, verifiability,
effectiveness.
Result/effect checks and entrypoint evidence are shared techniques; their accepted

Three retained rules concern the required artifact, unacceptable manual re-entry,

Post-fix transfer count: 3 tested; 1 specific, 2 partly shared, 0 transferable.
Nearest siblings in bullet order: functionality, usability, interoperability.

### 112 viability

The continuation gate is a supplied stakeholder model, not a market forecast or
a universal business-value definition.
The maple upkeep increase is labeled as a scenario before the revised fixture
cycle supplies its own evidence.
Removing legacy output is not claimed to cause recipient value.

The no-recipient/no-exit-duty assumption is essential to the example's reduction.
Real recipient commitments or missing ownership would keep continuation
conditional. Gate results recommend a decision and never approve renewal
or retirement automatically.

Post-fix transfer count: 4 tested; 2 specific, 2 partly shared, 0 transferable.
Nearest siblings in bullet order: feasibility, affordability, affordability,
maintainability.
Remaining costs and ownership overlap those concerns, but the accepted decision
is continued stakeholder value under ongoing obligations over a chosen horizon.
